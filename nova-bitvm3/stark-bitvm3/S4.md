# S4: the Stwo circuit verifier through the Boolean lowering

S3q cleared the native-proof blocker for the Shinigami standalone trace. S4 is the verifier side: how large is the pinned Stwo verifier once it is expressed as a Boolean relation, does that relation accept real proofs and reject changed ones, and can a Shinigami statement reach it? The answer to the last question is yes (S4e): the unmodified Shinigami executable is proved under upstream's privacy bootloader and the 4.78-billion-AND final verifier accepts its receipts.

The pins are the S2 pins: `starkware-libs/proving` at `e9a38e12d09979f7a8625e233a80fecac6c38f02`, imported through the S1 lowering (`stwo-bitvm3-lowering`, `stwo-bitvm3-upstream`). The checker is the crate `s4-cairo/` and the prover is `s4-prove/` (nightly-2026-01-15, no Nova, no SP1, no CUDA, no garbling).

## What upstream's verifier stack looks like

A Cairo proof is not verified by the Boolean relation directly. Upstream verifies it inside a circuit (`circuit-cairo-verifier`), proves that circuit with a circuit prover, and the last link is a **1-to-1 circuit verifier** (`circuit_verifier::multiverifier::build_multiverifier_circuit` with one input). That last circuit is small and proof-independent in shape, so it is the natural thing to turn into a Boolean relation. Its public statement is exactly two values: the preprocessed root of the proved circuit and the 8-word output digest. The Cairo verifier circuit exposes the program's Blake2s output digest, and the Cairo proof must carry exactly two 128-bit output cells holding it.

## Template sizes (proof-independent)

Non-free ANDs are the garbling cost (XOR is free). AND counts are exact, from `count.rs`: a per-constraint-shape count that matches the monolithic AND count to the last gate wherever both ran (both final-verifier rows). XOR counts differ by under 4,000 between the two counters; the final-verifier rows give the monolithic figure and the Cairo-verifier row, where only the per-shape counter ran, gives that one.

| Circuit | Config | Vars | Constraints | Raw input bits | Non-free ANDs | XORs |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Final 1-to-1 verifier, **production recursion config** (`PRIVACY_RECURSION_CIRCUIT_PREPROCESSED_ROOT`) | blowup 2, 35 queries, pow 26, fold 4, trace log 21 | 2,961,135 | 1,177,730 | 379,025,280 | **4,783,261,848** | 9,214,805,428 |
| Final 1-to-1 verifier, **fixture config** (the config of upstream's committed proof) | blowup 3, 23 queries, pow 27, fold 4, trace log 21 | 2,057,107 | 819,367 | 263,309,696 | **3,338,462,413** | 6,435,641,823 |
| Final 1-to-1 verifier, **laboratory profile L21** (Shinigami receipts, S4e) | the production recursion config | 2,961,135 | 1,177,730 | 379,025,280 | **4,783,261,848** | as the production row |
| Cairo verifier circuit (privacy bootloader, 2,584-word program, 57 components, zk blinding 45) | blowup 3 | 5,322,183 | 2,488,198 | 681,239,424 | 14,693,909,921 | 30,134,062,230 |

Gate mix of the production final verifier: add 112,760; sub 114,837; mul 128,016; pointwise-mul 43,851; eq 14,546; triple-xor 59,624; m31-to-u32 107,777; Blake-G 596,240; 70 permutations (widest 152, total width 9,310); 9 output gates. The fixture-config verifier has Blake-G 413,840 and 46 permutations. Blake-G gates dominate the ANDs, as expected from Merkle path hashing.

Consequences:

- At the S2 rate of 16 bytes per non-free AND, garbling the production final verifier is about 76.5 GB of ciphertext and the fixture-config one about 53.4 GB. The S2 measurement (141.6 million non-free gates, a 2.11 GiB ciphertext file, 24.7 s to garble) covers a test verifier 24 to 34 times smaller by AND count than these two; its timings and sizes are a lower bound, not a forecast.
- A Cairo program is **not** verified at the Boolean level directly. In the Cairo verifier circuit every program word costs about 142.4 thousand ANDs (measured by replacing the 2,584-word program with N words), so verifying a 169,856-word Shinigami executable as a fixed program would add about 24 billion ANDs on top of the 14.3 billion base, roughly 38.5 billion in total. The privacy bootloader (2,584 words) running the executable as a task, followed by the recursion into the final 1-to-1 verifier, keeps the Boolean statement at the 3.3 to 4.8 billion level for any program. That is the architecture BitVM3 should target, and it means Shinigami must be proved *under the bootloader* rather than as a standalone executable (done in S4e).

## Evidence: a real proof through the Boolean relation

`s4-cairo check` runs upstream's committed proof of the padded privacy Cairo-verifier circuit, `test_data/multiverifier/proof_cairo.bin` (182,900 bytes), through the final 1-to-1 verifier built with the **fixture config**. The proof configuration, the preprocessed root and the output digest come from upstream's fixture constants (`circuit_prover::test_utils`), never from the proof; the proof is deserialized against the pinned proof configuration and every byte must be consumed.

Local run (WSL, 20 threads, one shot, peak RSS 1.48 GB, 36 to 44 s wall):

| Step | Result |
|---|---|
| Native relation (`FinalizedContext::is_circuit_valid`) | accepted (0.1 s) |
| Proof-bearing circuit equals the proof-free template | yes (the template is a faithful topology) |
| Imported program | 2,057,107 variables, 819,367 constraints, 8 public output pins (since S4e computed from the statement, not read from the witness) |
| Boolean relation, sharded (410 shards of 2,000 constraints, 16 threads) | **accepted**, 4.4 to 6.9 s |
| Mutations of the proof (re-synthesizing every internal value against the same trusted template) | `trace_at_oods[0]+1`, `claimed_sums[0]+1`, `fri_last_layer_coef+1`, `oods_pow_nonce+1`, `fri_witness_first+1`, `channel_salt+1`: **all six rejected by the native relation and by the Boolean relation** |
| Non-canonical alias (a variable holding p instead of 0) | rejected by the Boolean relation |

CI reproduction: [run 42946737257](https://github.com/patrickdugan/garbled-snark-verifier/actions/runs/42946737257), job `s4-verifier-circuit`, commit `762cd77`, hosted runner (4 threads): the same acceptance, the same six rejected mutations and the alias rejection; Boolean relation 11.6 s, whole check 91.6 s, peak RSS 633 MB; every template count above identical to the local figures; the unit test passes. Artifact `stwo-s4-verifier-circuit-762cd77aedf312fd3681abe50be673446d3fc7b3` (zip digest `sha256:b04e2811fe0daec73e2dbdb9355db99f9f4696d21c953bb7c73e810c75de23b7`). A second run after the `check-prod` refactor, [run 42949797073](https://github.com/patrickdugan/garbled-snark-verifier/actions/runs/42949797073) (commit `2f67df3`), gives the same counts and verdicts.

Sharding: a program of 2.06 million variables is split into sub-programs over the variables each shard touches, each re-pinned. The conjunction of the shards equals the monolithic relation; a unit test (`shard::tests::sharding_never_changes_the_verdict`) checks equal verdicts for shard sizes 1 to 1000 and for every single-limb mutation of the all-gate fixture. The shard-summed ANDs (3,412,801,060) exceed the monolithic count (3,338,462,413) by 2.2% because shared variables are decoded in each shard that reads them; the monolithic count is the one that matters for garbling.

## S4c: a production-config proof through the Boolean relation

The committed `test_data/privacy/recursion_proof.bin` (43,300 bytes) is a recursion proof under `FriParams::test_params()`: deserializing it with the production proof configuration fails (`ValueOutOfRange`) and its root is not `PRIVACY_RECURSION_CIRCUIT_PREPROCESSED_ROOT`, so it cannot serve as production evidence. A production-config proof was therefore generated with upstream's own pipeline. `s4-prove/` builds against the pinned revision (WSL, `-C target-cpu=native`, 5.5 minutes cold) and runs the privacy bootloader on upstream's fixture PIE `privacy_tx_cairo_pie.zip`, proves the Cairo execution (production Cairo profile: blowup 3, 23 queries, pow 27, trace log 20), proves the Cairo-verifier circuit (production circuit profile: blowup 2, 35 queries, pow 26, trace log 21), verifies the result with upstream's `verify_recursive_circuit`, and stores the raw recursion proof and the 32-byte output digest.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Prover wall clock, whole pipeline | 17.8 to 18.8 s on 20 cores (Cairo proof 8.7 s, recursion proof 15.5 s) |
| Prover peak RSS (`/usr/bin/time -v`) | 14.3 to 14.6 GB (WSL limit 20 GB) |
| Emitted proof | 226,416 bytes (35-byte header plus zstd); raw recursion proof 263,812 bytes |
| Bootloader output | 9 felts, digest `1f5972852af477379b1a3dd50f4dea0e1b05c5e84647ce155c032b907b8e9cb4`, root = the production constant |
| Determinism | two runs byte-identical; the generalized `s4-prove` pipeline is byte-identical to `privacy_recursive_prove` |

`s4-cairo check-prod` then runs this proof through the same checks as the fixture proof, against the **production** template (blowup 2, 35 queries; 2,961,135 variables, 1,177,730 constraints, equal to the template counted above):

- native relation accepted (0.1 s);
- Boolean relation, sharded (589 shards, 16 threads): **accepted**, 8.1 s, summed ANDs 4,889,964,208 (2.2% over the monolithic 4,783,261,848 because of re-decoded shared variables);
- the same six mutations and the non-canonical alias: all rejected by the native and by the Boolean relation;
- whole check 54.8 s, peak RSS 1.56 GB.

So the production final 1-to-1 verifier, as a Boolean relation over its full 4.78 billion non-free ANDs, accepts a production proof and rejects changes. This check is local only: the prover needs 14.6 GB, which a hosted runner does not have, so CI covers the fixture config.

## S4d: Shinigami under the privacy bootloader exceeds the production small profile

The Scarb executable has a `Bootloader` entrypoint (169,856 bytecode words; builtins output, range_check, bitwise), so it can be handed to the privacy bootloader as a Cairo-1 task. `s4-prove` does so (the upstream function takes only a PIE and keeps its helpers private, so the pipeline body is repeated, verified byte-identical on the PIE). The bootloader executes the task, but under the production profile the Cairo prover panics in the twiddle table ("Not enough twiddles!"), because the trace exceeds the fixed 2^20-row profile. The adapter's dimensions show why; the cost is the **program length**, not the execution:

| Run | Steps | Memory cells | Notes |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Shinigami standalone (S3) | 157,303 | 378,312 max address | the S3q proof |
| Fixture PIE under the privacy bootloader | 275,860 | 328,836 | proves in 18 s |
| Shinigami as a task, program hash Blake | 2,770,649 | 3,008,238 | production profile rejects the size; proved in S4e |
| Shinigami as a task, program hash Poseidon | 1,093,754 | 1,815,096 | dimensions only |
| Shinigami as a task, program hash Pedersen | 1,518,384 | 2,018,918 | dimensions only |

With Blake the run costs 17.6 times the standalone steps and 7.9 times the standalone memory. The component that breaks the cap is `blake_g`, measured at log size 21 (23,017 Blake compressions; ten rounds of eight G calls each is 1,841,360 rows). Every other component of the Blake run stays at or below 2^20 (measured component log sizes in S4e). The task also never touches the Pedersen or Poseidon builtins, so 11 of the 57 components of the production set are absent and the production Cairo-verifier circuit, whose component set is fixed, would reject the proof even at the right size. The registry's large-proof flow (`privacy_large_proofs`, blowup 1, 70 queries) only has leaf verifiers for trace log sizes 25 to 29 and lifts to the 2^25 preprocessed height, far beyond this machine.

## S4e: Shinigami receipts through the Boolean relation (laboratory profile L21)

The full, unmodified Shinigami executable of S3 (SHA-256 `a9bdc409d2f7ee443ac62cabdc8df27fb44c85a435ca9350788bee890f61bef3`, the S3 pin) is proved as a task of upstream's privacy bootloader, recursed into a 1-to-1 circuit proof, and that proof is accepted by the Boolean relation of the final verifier. Four receipts are covered: an accepting and a rejecting one for each of the two script profiles.

### The profile

`s4-cairo/src/lab_config.rs` pins laboratory profile L21. It changes three things relative to upstream's production privacy profile and nothing else:

| | Production privacy profile | L21 |
|---|---|---|
| Largest Cairo component | 2^20 rows | 2^21 rows |
| Cairo proof FRI | blowup 3, 23 queries, 27 proof-of-work bits | blowup 2, 35 queries, 26 proof-of-work bits |
| Cairo trees lifted to | 2^23 | 2^23 |
| Cairo components | 57 | 46 (the Pedersen and Poseidon families are absent) |
| Bootloader and program hash | privacy bootloader, 2,584 words, Blake | same |
| Preprocessed trace | canonical small | same |
| Interaction proof of work, zero-knowledge blinding | 24 bits, 45 | same |
| Circuit proof FRI | blowup 2, 35 queries, 26 bits | same |
| Cairo-verifier circuit | trace log 21, 45 preprocessed columns | trace log 21, the **identical** 45-column layout |
| Final 1-to-1 verifier | 4,783,261,848 non-free ANDs | the same circuit, gate for gate |

Both FRI triples are upstream's own `privacy_pcs_config` pairs for its 96-bit target (`pow_bits + n_queries * log_blowup >= 96`). Because the L21 Cairo-verifier circuit pads to the same component sizes as the production one, the final verifier's proof configuration is the production one: `s4-cairo final-lab` counts the same 2,961,135 variables, 1,177,730 constraints and 4,783,261,848 non-free ANDs. Only one of its two public values differs, the preprocessed root of the verified circuit.

Two roots are pinned, and neither is trusted from a proof:

- The Cairo preprocessed root (canonical small, blowup 2, lifted to 2^23) is outside upstream's table, which assumes trace log 20. `s4-prove lab-roots` recomputes it from the preprocessed trace alone, after showing that the same procedure reproduces upstream's table entry `get_preprocessed_root(23)` for blowup 3.
- The root of the L21 Cairo-verifier circuit is recomputed by `s4-cairo lab-root` from the profile constants alone (2.2 s, 1.5 GB) and must equal the pinned constant.

### The pipeline

Each stage is its own process (`s4-prove`), so the Cairo prover and the circuit prover never share memory. All figures are WSL on this laptop (20 threads, 20 GB limit), over the four cases.

| Stage | What it does | Time | Peak RSS |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `lab-cairo` | privacy bootloader runs the task (2,770,649 steps, 3,008,238 memory cells), Cairo proof, native Cairo verifier (`verify_cairo_ex`) accepts | 22.6 to 25.0 s proof, 24 to 31 s stage | 15.1 to 15.4 GB |
| `lab-recurse` | builds the pinned L21 Cairo-verifier circuit, requires the proof's component set, PCS configuration and preprocessed root to equal the pins, the circuit accepts the Cairo proof, circuit proof, native 1-to-1 verifier accepts | 9.1 to 12.4 s proof, 8 to 14 s stage | 8.7 to 9.2 GB |
| `lab-bind` | recomputes the task's Blake program hash from the executable and finds it in the bootloader output | 3 s | under 20 MB |
| `s4-cairo check-lab` | native relation, Boolean relation, mutations (below) | 82 to 105 s | 1.6 GB |

Measured component log sizes of the Cairo proof, in component order: `[19, 20, 18, 14, 18, 20, 15, 4, 12, 13, 18, 4, 4, 9, 18, 15, 18, 12, 14, 18, 21, 4, 18, 20, 12, 18, 18, 16, 20, 6, 8, 11, 12, 18, 20, 7, 8, 18, 14, 18, 16, 15, 8, 14, 16, 18]`; the single 21 is `blake_g`. The Cairo proof is 776 KB compressed; the raw recursion proof is 263,812 bytes, the same size as the production one.

### The statement

The final verifier takes two public values. For L21 they are derived by the checker, not supplied by the prover:

1. **Root**: the pinned constant `CIRCUIT_PREPROCESSED_ROOT`.
2. **Digest**: Blake2s over the bootloader output preimage, recomputed by the checker from `output_preimage.json`. The checker requires the preimage to be `[1, n + 2, program_hash, n receipt words]` with `program_hash` equal to the pinned `SHINIGAMI_PROGRAM_HASH` (`0x35d865e3a2c16e04032630546acb93f8d1b883cbb6fb688eb4e1afd1dc42f8`), which `lab-bind` recomputes with the bootloader's `compute_program_hash_chain` over the 169,856-word executable. This resolves the open point of S3 about the meaning of the three-cell prefix.

The final verifier's own eight output words are then Blake2s over the verified circuit's hash (configuration and root) followed by the digest. The checker computes them natively from the statement (mirroring upstream's `test_verify_circuit_single_input_output_digest`) and pins them in the Boolean program. No public value is read from the witness.

For every case:

- the native Cairo verifier, the Cairo-verifier circuit, the native 1-to-1 verifier and the native relation of the final verifier accept;

- the honest witness is rejected by the Boolean relation pinned to a statement with one digest bit flipped, and to one with one root bit flipped;
- a witness synthesized for another digest is rejected by the native and by the Boolean relation pinned to the true statement;
- a non-canonical alias (p for 0) is rejected.

A rejecting receipt is a statement like any other: the Shinigami engine ran and said no, and that verdict is what the digest commits to. Two independent cross-checks on `csv_valid`: its 21 receipt words equal the standalone S3 output word for word, and its txid equals SHA256d of the raw transaction computed outside the stack.

The four recursion proofs and their preimages are committed under `s4-cairo/fixtures/shinigami/` (SHA-256 in `SHA256SUMS`), so `check-lab` runs in CI without the 15 GB prover. [Run 42957651998](https://github.com/patrickdugan/garbled-snark-verifier/actions/runs/42957651998) (commit `64bdaf4`, hosted runner, 4 threads) recomputed the pinned circuit root, counted the same 4,783,261,848 ANDs and accepted all four receipts through the Boolean relation, with the full mutation set on `csv_valid` (127 s, 0.8 GB) and the statement mutations on the other three (59 s each). Proving is deterministic: a second full run reproduced all four recursion proofs byte for byte.

## What this establishes, and what it does not

Established, at the pinned revisions:

1. The Boolean size of the production final verifier (4.78 billion non-free ANDs, 379 million raw input bits, 2.96 million variables) and of the Cairo verifier circuit, as exact, reproducible counts.
2. A Boolean lowering of a circuit-proof verifier **accepts real proofs** (upstream's committed fixture proof, a production-config recursion proof, and four Shinigami recursion proofs) and rejects proof mutations, statement mutations and a non-canonical alias each time.
3. **A Shinigami statement reaches the Boolean relation.** The unmodified 169,856-word Shinigami executable is proved under upstream's privacy bootloader on a laptop, and the 4.78-billion-AND final verifier accepts receipts that bind the program hash, the request, the verdict and the txid.
4. The architecture: the Cairo program stays out of the Boolean statement, and the Boolean circuit is the same for every program and every receipt; only two public values change.

Not established:

- L21 is a laboratory profile. Each parameter is an upstream constant or an upstream parameter pair, but the combination (2^21 Cairo trace, blowup 2, 46 components) is not an upstream-reviewed configuration, and no soundness claim is made beyond upstream's own formula.
- In S4 the Boolean relation is evaluated directly, in shards. Garbling at this size is S5 ([S5.md](S5.md)); no Bitcoin script exists and the dispute protocol is untouched.
- The receipt is the S3 pilot: regtest domain, the CSV and SHA256-hashlock profiles, no signature opcodes.
- The program-hash pin is as reproducible as the Scarb 2.18.0 build of the pinned sources; CI rebuilds that executable in the S3 jobs but does not re-derive the hash from it.
- Proof generation is local only (15.4 GB); CI checks the committed proofs.

## Reproduce

```bash
# WSL or Linux, nightly-2026-01-15
cd nova-bitvm3/stark-bitvm3/s4-cairo
cargo build --release && cargo test --release
BIN=target/release/stwo-bitvm3-s4-cairo
FIX=$(find ~/.cargo/git/checkouts -type d -path '*/e9a38e1*/test_data/multiverifier' | head -1)
$BIN check "$FIX"                       # upstream's committed proof: native + Boolean + mutations
$BIN final --analytic                   # production final verifier template
$BIN final-fixture --boolean --analytic
$BIN cairo --analytic                   # Cairo verifier circuit (add --program-words N)

# Shinigami receipts, laboratory profile L21 (committed fixtures; about 1.6 GB, 2 minutes each)
$BIN lab-root                           # recompute the pinned circuit root from the profile
$BIN final-lab --analytic               # the final verifier of L21 (equals the production one)
for c in csv_valid csv_below_delay hashlock_valid hashlock_wrong_preimage; do
  $BIN check-lab fixtures/shinigami/$c/recursion_raw.bin fixtures/shinigami/$c/output_preimage.json
done

# Regenerate the proofs (WSL, 15.4 GB peak; separate crate, RUSTFLAGS='-C target-cpu=native')
cd ../s4-prove && cargo build --release
P=target/release/stwo-bitvm3-s4-prove
EXE=../s3-shinigami/target/dev/shinigami_bitvm3_receipt.executable.json   # scarb build in s3-shinigami
ARGS=../s3-shinigami/out/csv_valid.args.json                              # tools/cases.py writes these
$P lab-roots OUT && $P lab-cairo $EXE OUT $ARGS && $P lab-bind $EXE OUT $ARGS && $P lab-recurse OUT
# production profile on upstream's fixture PIE (14.6 GB): $P PIE.zip OUT, then $BIN check-prod OUT/recursion_raw.bin OUT/output_digest.hex
```

CI runs the checker side through the opt-in `s4-verifier-circuit` job (`[s4-check]` in a push commit message, or the `s4_verifier_circuit` dispatch input). Results are in [S4_RESULTS.json](S4_RESULTS.json).
